## The wrong question

Most people ask "can I afford an agency?" The more useful question is **"what is doing this myself actually costing me?"**

If you are spending fifteen hours a week on ad accounts, catalogue updates, and courier disputes, that time has a price — and for a founder it is usually the highest-value time in the business. Fifteen hours a week not spent on product, sourcing, or partnerships is the real cost, and it does not appear on any invoice.

## Stay in-house when

- **You are below roughly ₹3 lakh a month in net sales.** At that level most agency fee structures take a meaningful bite, and the work is small enough to handle yourself.
- **You are still finding product-market fit.** Nobody can market their way out of a product people do not want. Spend the money on the product.
- **You genuinely enjoy it and have the time.** Founders who run their own ads well often outperform agencies early, because they know the customer better than anyone they could hire.
- **Your channel mix is simple.** One marketplace, or one store with one ad platform, is manageable solo.

## Hire an agency when

- **The work has outgrown your calendar.** The clearest signal is things quietly not getting done — campaigns left running past their usefulness, listings not updated, RTO disputes never filed.
- **You need several skills at once.** Media buying, creative, catalogue, logistics, and reporting are different competencies. Hiring one person gets you one of them well.
- **You are scaling and the mistakes are getting expensive.** At ₹50,000 a month in ad spend, a 20% efficiency gap is ₹10,000. At ₹5,00,000, it is ₹1,00,000.
- **You are entering a channel you do not know.** Learning Amazon or Flipkart from scratch while running your existing business is a slow, costly education.

## Hire an employee instead when

- **The need is deep rather than broad.** If you need serious performance-marketing capability and nothing else, one strong hire beats an agency.
- **You have enough volume to keep them busy.** Below a certain scale a full-time marketer has idle capacity you are paying for.
- **You want the knowledge to stay inside the business.** Agencies leave. Employees, sometimes, do not.

## The arithmetic

At ₹5,00,000 monthly net sales:

| Option | Monthly cost | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Do it yourself | ₹0 cash | 10–20 hours of founder time |
| Employee | ₹35,000–₹80,000 + overhead | One skill set, full attention |
| Agency (retainer) | ₹75,000+ | Several skill sets, split attention |
| Agency (3.5% of net) | ₹17,500 | Several skill sets, split attention |

The percentage model changes the arithmetic materially at smaller scale — which is precisely why we chose it. At ₹5 lakh net sales, ₹17,500 is close to what a good freelancer costs and considerably less than a retainer.

## Questions to ask before signing anything

- **Who will actually do the work?** Get the name. If the answer is vague, the answer is a junior.
- **What happens in a bad month?** Their answer tells you which pricing model you are really buying.
- **Can I leave?** Ask about notice period, exit fee, and account handover specifically.
- **Do I keep ownership of the accounts?** If an agency wants your store or ad accounts registered under their name, walk away.
- **What do you need from me?** An agency that says "nothing, we handle everything" has not run an account before. They will need product images, margin data, and stock visibility.
