# Module 6 — Copywriting System

**Status:** Complete draft
**Standard:** B (specification)
**Depends on:** `LOCKED.md` §1, §2, §4; Module 3 (section copy); Module 5 (proof language)
**Governs:** every word on the site — headlines, body, CTAs, nav labels, form
text, error states, meta descriptions, WhatsApp pre-fills, alt text

---

## §1 — The Voice

### Who is writing

A senior ecommerce operator who has run these accounts, explaining something to
a founder they respect. Not a marketer, not a consultant, not a coach.
(LOCKED#11.)

The test for any sentence: **would a person who has actually done this work say
it out loud to another person?** "We leverage data-driven strategies to
maximise your ROI" fails. "We look at what happens after the sale before we
decide to buy more of them" passes.

### The four voice properties

**Plain.** Shortest true word wins. "Use" not "utilise." "Buy" not "acquire."
"Now" not "at this juncture."

**Specific.** Numbers, names, mechanisms. "A courier that's ₹15 cheaper but
delivers 8% less often" beats "suboptimal logistics partners."

**Calm.** No exclamation marks anywhere on the site. No capitals for emphasis.
No urgency. The target emotion is relief (LOCKED#12) — anxious copy cannot
produce a calm reader.

**Unflattering when necessary.** The copy is allowed to say things that cost
money: the product isn't ready, the price is wrong, you're too small for this
model. This is the property competitors can't copy, because it requires being
willing to lose the sale.

### Voice in one line

> Write like you're explaining it to a founder you like, who is smart but new
> to this, and who will notice if you're selling.

## §2 — The Language Rule

The hardest constraint on this site and the one that most differentiates it.

**Every technical term is explained the first time it appears on any page.**
Not in a glossary, not in a tooltip — inline, in the same sentence or the next.

The reader may be running a successful offline business and have never
encountered "RTO," "ROAS," "CAC," "pixel," "attribution," or "AOV." A
sophisticated founder skims the explanation and loses two seconds. An
unsophisticated one, without it, loses the page.

### The pattern

**Term → plain restatement → why it matters.**

> RTO stands for Return to Origin — the parcel goes out, the customer refuses
> it at the door, and it comes back to you. You've already paid to acquire that
> customer and paid shipping both ways.

> A 5.1x return on ad spend means for every ₹1 spent on ads, ₹5.10 came back in
> tracked sales.

> Net sales is what's left after refunds, returns and RTOs come out — the money
> that actually stayed in your account.

### Terms requiring explanation on first use

| Term | Approved plain-language restatement |
|---|---|
| RTO | Return to Origin — the customer refused delivery and the parcel came back to you |
| Return | The customer received it, then sent it back — not the same as RTO |
| ROAS / return on ad spend | For every ₹1 spent on ads, ₹X came back in tracked sales |
| Net sales | What's left after refunds, returns and RTOs — the money that actually stayed |
| COD | Cash on Delivery — the customer pays the courier, not you, and nothing upfront |
| Prepaid | Paid online before dispatch |
| CAC | What it costs you in ads to get one paying customer |
| Conversion rate | Out of every 100 people who visit, how many actually buy |
| AOV | The average value of one order |
| Pixel / tracking | The code that tells Meta or Google which ads led to actual sales |
| Listing | The product page on Amazon, Flipkart or Meesho |
| Catalogue | All your product listings on a marketplace, together |
| Retainer | A fixed monthly fee, charged whether or not you sold anything |

### Terms to avoid entirely

Not explainable in a way that earns its space. Rewrite around them: *funnel,
top-of-funnel, LTV, attribution window, incrementality, MER, blended ROAS,
creative fatigue, audience saturation, omnichannel, synergy, holistic,
leverage (as verb), unlock, supercharge, game-changing, best-in-class,
cutting-edge, seamless, robust, bespoke, curated, elevate, empower.*

## §3 — Vocabulary

Locked terms. These are the words the company owns (LOCKED#27) — using them
consistently is itself a positioning act.

| Say | Never say |
|---|---|
| Partner Brand | Client, customer |
| What We Own | Services, offerings, solutions |
| Start on WhatsApp | Contact Us, Get in Touch, Book a Call |
| Founder | User, lead, prospect |
| Net sales | Revenue (when precision matters) |
| We take ownership | We provide / we offer / we deliver |
| Decision | Strategy, tactic, initiative |
| Fee | Pricing package, plan, tier |
| Guide | Blog post, article, content |
| Refused at the door | Failed delivery |

### Naming conventions

- The company is **Orcas Prime**, never "Orcas," never "OP," never "we at Orcas Prime."
- Currency: `₹6,70,173` — Indian numbering with lakh grouping, symbol not "Rs" or "INR", no space after the symbol.
- Large figures: `₹13.2 lakh` in prose, exact figures in proof blocks. Never "13.2L" in body copy.
- Percentages: `3.5%`, never "3.5 percent."
- Platforms capitalised exactly: Shopify, Meta, Google Ads, Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, WhatsApp.

## §4 — Sentence & Structure Rules

**Sentences.** Average under 18 words. Vary length deliberately — a short
sentence after two long ones creates the emphasis that bold text would
otherwise be doing.

**Paragraphs.** Three sentences maximum on mobile. Four on desktop. White space
between paragraphs is doing readability work for a reader on a phone in poor
light.

**No bold for emphasis inside body copy.** Bold is reserved for sub-headings
only. If a sentence needs bolding to land, the sentence is wrong.

**No bullet lists in persuasive sections.** Sections 2, 3, 5 and 8 are
conversations (LOCKED#20) — bullets turn an argument into a spec sheet.
Lists are permitted only in Section 4's ownership table, Section 7's guide
cards, and the footer.

**Second person throughout.** "Your store," "you've already paid," "your
courier." First person plural for Orcas Prime — "we look at," "we'd tell you."
Never third person ("Orcas Prime helps brands to...").

**Open loops.** Every section's final line points forward (LOCKED#33). Never
close a section with a summary of itself.

## §5 — Headline Patterns

Four patterns that work for this brand. Anything else needs a reason.

**1. The recognised reality** — describes the founder's situation back to them.
> You're the founder, the marketer, the ops manager, and the person filing courier complaints — all before lunch.

**2. The structural truth** — names how the system actually works.
> Nothing about Indian ecommerce is designed to be run by one person.

**3. The stated difference** — what we do that others don't, without naming them.
> Most agencies tell you what they did. We'll tell you why we decided it.

**4. The plain fact** — no persuasion at all, just the thing.
> Every number below is from an account we're running right now.

### Headline rules

- Never a question as a headline. Questions read as ad copy.
- Never a headline that could belong to another agency. Test: swap in a
  competitor's name — if it still works, rewrite it.
- Sentence case, not Title Case. Full stops permitted and preferred.
- Under 14 words. If it won't fit, the subhead is doing work the headline should.

## §6 — CTA Library

Locked: never "Contact Us" (LOCKED#35), never urgency (LOCKED#36).

### Primary CTA

`Start on WhatsApp →`

Used sitewide. Pre-filled message varies by context:

| Context | Pre-filled message |
|---|---|
| Homepage hero & Section 8 | Hi, I want to check if the 3.5% model fits my store. |
| Pricing page | Hi, I want to understand the 3.5% fee for my store. |
| What We Own page | Hi, I want to know what you'd take over for my brand. |
| Amazon wedge offer | Hi, I want to start selling on Amazon with the free 3-month onboarding. |
| A guide page | Hi, I read your guide on [topic] and want to talk about my store. |

### Secondary CTAs (text links)

`See how the fee works →` · `See what we'd take over →` · `Read the full RTO guide →` ·
`See every Partner Brand →` · `How the 3 months works →`

### Never

*Get Started · Learn More · Submit · Book a Free Consultation · Claim Your Free
Audit · Get Your Free Quote · Contact Us · Talk to an Expert · Schedule a Call ·
Sign Up · Join Now · Limited Slots · Book Before [date]*

### CTA rules

- Every CTA states what happens next, not what the visitor should feel.
- Arrow `→` on every CTA, no other icon.
- One primary CTA per screen. Never two competing buttons side by side.
- No CTA before Section 6 on the homepage (LOCKED#24 — trust earned progressively).

## §7 — Microcopy

The small text that carries disproportionate trust weight.

| Location | Copy |
|---|---|
| Under the primary CTA | No forms, no discovery call booking, no sales sequence. A conversation. |
| Under the pricing table | 3.5% + GST of net sales. No setup fee, no onboarding fee, no creative fee, no reporting fee. |
| Next to ad-spend mention | You pay Meta and Google directly, exactly what they charge. We never mark up your ad spend. |
| Next to account access | We run ads inside your own ad accounts, not ours. You keep the data, the history and the access. |
| Under proof figures | Tracked revenue from live accounts. Figures current as of [month, year]. |
| Under the Amazon offer | Amazon pays us a referral commission for bringing new sellers on. That's how it works, and that's why it's free. |
| Guide index | No email required, no gated PDFs. If it would help you run your business better, it should just be readable. |
| Footer, near address | Jaipur, Rajasthan. |
| 404 page | That page doesn't exist. Here's what does: [nav links] |

### Microcopy rules

- Every fee statement includes what is *not* charged. Absence of hidden costs is
  a trust asset and must be stated, not implied.
- Every metric carries its period.
- Never "*terms and conditions apply" or asterisked qualifications. If a
  condition matters, write it in the sentence.

## §8 — Page-Level Copy Direction

Module 3 specifies homepage copy in full. This covers the remaining pages.

### What We Own

**H1:** You build the product. We build the business around it.
**Structure:** the 14-item ownership table from Module 3 §4, expanded — each item
gets two or three sentences on what it actually involves day to day.
**Tone:** operational and unglamorous. This page's job is specificity, not
persuasion. A founder should finish it able to picture their week changing.
**Ends with:** the trust qualifier (own ad accounts, no markup), then CTA.

### Pricing

**H1:** One fee. Charged on what you actually keep.
**Must contain:** the three-way comparison (retainer / % of ad spend / % of net
sales) written as a genuine explanation of incentives, not as a competitor
attack (LOCKED#26). The table from Module 3 §6. The honest-fit block
(₹3-50 lakh). The exit terms once defined (Module 5 §10).
**Must not contain:** tiers, packages, "most popular" badges, feature-comparison
matrices, or the word "plan."

### Work

**H1:** Every brand we've worked with, and what we decided.
**Structure:** three case studies in the Module 5 §4 format. Growth Decision Log
entries. Testimonials last.
**Tone:** reporting, not celebrating. Include what's still in progress for each
brand — that section is what makes the rest believable.

### About

**H1:** Why we built Orcas Prime this way.
**Structure:** the belief (`LOCKED.md` §1 — the future of ecommerce agencies is
not selling services, it's taking ownership), then the founders with real
backgrounds, then the Principles.
**Tone:** the most personal page on the site. First person is permitted here in
a way it isn't elsewhere.
**Must not contain:** "our mission is," "founded in 2024 with a vision,"
"passionate team of experts."

### Guides (Module 7)

**H1 pattern:** the question a founder would actually type or ask.
*What is RTO, and why is it so high in India?* — not *"Understanding RTO: A
Comprehensive Guide."*
**Opening:** answer the question in the first two sentences. No throat-clearing,
no "in today's competitive ecommerce landscape."
**Ends with:** the natural next step, not a pitch (LOCKED#41) — *"This is how
we'd approach it for your store."*

## §9 — Meta & Machine-Facing Copy

Read by search engines, AI systems, and social previews. Often the first copy
anyone sees.

**Title tag pattern:** `[What the page is] — Orcas Prime`
Homepage: `Ecommerce growth partner for Indian brands — Orcas Prime`
Current title leads with location and price; it should lead with what the
company does, mirroring the hero change.

**Meta description:** one plain sentence stating what the page contains. No
keyword stuffing, no "Contact us today!"
Homepage: `We run your Shopify store, ads and marketplace accounts for 3.5% of net sales. No retainer. Based in Jaipur, working with Indian ecommerce brands.`

**Open Graph:** same as meta description. OG image is the wordmark on `--paper`,
no stock imagery, no text-heavy banner.

**Alt text:** describes what the image shows and why it's there. Partner Brand
screenshots: `Ladakh Berry storefront — seabuckthorn products, Shopify` not
`screenshot1`.

**`llms.txt`** (Module 7 owns the file, this owns its voice): plain factual
statements, no marketing language. What Orcas Prime is, who it serves, what it
charges, where the key pages are. Written to be quoted accurately, not to persuade.
